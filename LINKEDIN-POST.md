What can AI help us explore in a brain MRI?

I’ve been building BrainLens—a research website that makes MRI results easier to inspect and understand.

Currently, it supports:
• Suggested tumour categories for supported 2D MRI slices.
• 3D MRI viewing.
• Candidate-region overlays and volume measurements for compatible scans.
• Editable research review summaries.

The video shows a saved example where BrainLens suggests “Meningioma,” matching the dataset’s recorded label. That’s one example—not proof of reliable diagnostic accuracy.

BrainLens remains a research prototype. Its predictions are not confirmed diagnoses, a negative result does not establish a healthy brain, and experimental 2D tumour outlines remain offline.

The next milestone is independent evaluation with properly separated patient data.

I’d welcome feedback from medical-imaging researchers, radiologists, ML engineers, and anyone interested in making research tools easier to use.
