For axis-aligned files, axial, coronal and sagittal labels and proportions come from recorded coordinates. Oblique images use storage directions. 3. Run the local imaging model

Checking model availability…

The model marks candidate regions associated with gliomas, a specific brain tumor family. The markings are research output for review. Other diseases remain unassessed. Loading an example only opens its images; this step runs the actual imaging model.

SegResNet automatically uses the verified local GPU when it is available, with the CPU available as a fallback. The same public study took 51 seconds on the GPU and about 9 minutes on the CPU while training ran alongside it. Both runs produced identical markings. Swin UNETR uses the CPU and earlier public-study runs took about 9–16 minutes. Study size, device and other running work affect the time. Faster processing and a larger model do not establish better medical accuracy. Both choices use the official pretrained weights. See the separate training experiments and their measured errors.

Why are there different image sets?

Each MRI technique changes how the same tissue appears. The model uses all four together. These short explanations describe typical appearances; they cannot identify the correct technique from an image or determine whether a finding is disease.

How must my own files be prepared?

Use de-identified, permitted research images that are aligned, skull-stripped, and recorded at 1 mm spacing. Skull-stripped means tissue outside the brain was removed during preparation. This app does not perform those preparation steps.

File checks inspect dimensions, recorded positions, spacing, and intensities. They cannot verify sequence labels, patient identity, actual alignment, or skull stripping. Prepared BraTS-format research studies are an example of the expected input.

    What do the three tumour-result states mean?
    • Tumour suspected: would require an evaluated detection model and a supported decision rule. This state is not enabled for the installed models.
    • No tumour detected by the model: would require the same validation. It could still miss a tumour and would not mean a healthy brain. This state is not enabled.
    • Inconclusive / unable to evaluate: no supported tumour-presence decision is available, including when inputs are incompatible, inference fails or detection evidence is inadequate. This is the current tumour-assessment state even after successful region marking.

    The earlier classifier matched 12/18 reference labels; additional training matched 11/18. Both missed 5 of 10 tumour-reference cases. Neither is used to give you a tumour-presence answer.

    Can this tell me the tumour type or whether my brain is healthy?

    No. Region labels do not name a tumour, and the installed models do not assess all brain abnormalities. No separately evaluated normal-versus-abnormal classifier is installed. Candidate regions and an empty mask both require qualified review.
