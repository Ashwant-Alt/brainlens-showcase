What does this project do?

It helps you explore images from a brain MRI examination and inspect regions marked by a research model. It also shows how those markings compare with expert annotations in a public dataset.

A marking is not a diagnosis. The models were developed for glioma-region research. They do not check for every brain condition, and an empty result does not mean a healthy brain. This project has not established clinical reliability.

Three things you can do

  1. Explore an MRI. A 3D scan is a stack of thin images called slices. The slider lets you move through that stack.
  2. Run an imaging model. The model uses four image sets from the same examination. These image sets make tissue appear differently; the public examples provide them for you.
  3. Inspect the evidence. Compare candidate model markings with the dataset's expert-drawn regions. The report includes tissue the model missed and markings outside the reference.

You can also write and export review notes. An optional language AI can draft text from measured region volumes; it does not interpret the scans or improve the imaging model's accuracy.

What does the score mean?

We use a measure called Dice overlap. It compares two marked regions: the model's prediction and the expert annotation. A score of 1 means the regions are identical. A score of 0 means they do not overlap.

89.66% overlap does not mean 89.66% correct diagnoses. It measures agreement between shapes, not whether the model correctly identifies a person's disease. Expert annotations are the dataset reference, not a complete record of every medical problem.

What remains unproven?

  • Five studies are a small development comparison. Overlap with the original pretrained models' training data is unknown.
  • The examples contain gliomas. We have not measured false alarms on healthy brains or performance across other conditions.
  • We have not established clinical accuracy, calibrated uncertainty, regulatory approval or patient benefit.
  • The website currently reviews one examination. Comparing earlier and later examinations is future work.
  • Matching image headers does not prove actual anatomical alignment, sequence identity or patient identity.

A few MRI words, explained

MRI study, image set and slice

A study is the collection of images from one examination. An image set uses one acquisition technique. A slice is one thin section through the scan.

T1, T1 with contrast, T2 and FLAIR

These are different ways of acquiring MRI images so tissue appears differently. T1 with contrast is acquired after a contrast agent. FLAIR suppresses much of the free-fluid signal. The model uses the four techniques together; their names do not tell you whether a finding is disease.

Segmentation

Segmentation means marking a region in an image. It does not, by itself, establish a medical diagnosis.

NIfTI and DICOM

NIfTI (.nii or .nii.gz) is a research format that stores an image stack and its recorded positions. DICOM is commonly used by hospitals. This app currently supports prepared NIfTI data.

How the model and check work

This separate research experiment uses two MRI image sets: FLAIR and T1 with contrast. It checks original scan geometry, extracts a predicted brain boundary, attempts to align the images, then applies a frozen imaging encoder and a separately trained three-category classifier. The brain boundary is not a tumour outline.

Only the small classification layer was trained on 67 cases. Fifteen already known validation cases selected its training checkpoint and score scaling. The 26 public cases were excluded from fitting and selection. Actual imaging weights stayed fixed during this check; no external language API interpreted the scans.

Predictions and failures were saved before this scoring step opened clinical references. The developer had previously reviewed source metadata, and overlap with the encoder's original training patients remains unverified. This is an implementation and public-source research assessment by the same operator, not an external audit or a blinded clinical trial.

The original verifier used a different nearest-neighbour resampling path from the preparation pipeline. Its repair checks the actual saved path and the already declared coverage threshold. Existing scans, transforms, weights and original outcomes were preserved. Genuine alignment failures were not retried to obtain a match.

The encoder's downloaded weight metadata does not state redistribution permission. Those weights remain local research artifacts and are not included in a public release. The separate brain-extraction weights permit noncommercial use.

The older four-image-set classifier matched 12 / 19 cases (63.2%) in its earlier test and matched none of three oligodendroglioma references. Those were different patients and inputs. The new result cannot establish an improvement on the same cases or superiority over other models.

What is available in the website? Its imaging workflow still uses the published region-marking models. This new category experiment remains offline; it has not been added as a diagnostic result. The evidence helps decide what to improve next.
